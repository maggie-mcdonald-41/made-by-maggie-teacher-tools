Used in sentence starters like: “According to [Title]...”

👩‍🏫 Teacher Tips

Goal: Students use a clear claim, relevant evidence, and reasoning that explains how the evidence supports the claim.

Best use: This organizer works well for ELA, science, and social studies responses.

Example (Teacher model): The narrator feels embarrassed because she is worried that Robert will judge her family. According to Fish Cheeks, “I was stunned into silence for the rest of the night.” This evidence supports the claim because her silence shows how uncomfortable and ashamed she feels during the dinner. Her reaction helps explain why she is so focused on how others might view her family’s traditions.

Important: This tool provides structure, sentence starters, and feedback — it does not write answers for students.

CER Strategy
Claim • Evidence • Reasoning
1. Claim

Answer the question clearly. Make a direct statement that responds to the prompt.

2. Evidence

Use a quote, detail, fact, data point, or accurate paraphrase from the text, source, or investigation.

3. Reasoning

Explain how the evidence proves or supports your claim. This is where students do the thinking.

C — Claim: Restate + Answer

Begin by turning the question into a statement. Then add your clear answer or conclusion. Your claim should make sense even if the reader cannot see the original question.

Question or prompt you are answering
Your question or prompt will appear here.
1. Find the key words

Identify the important person, topic, event, relationship, or idea named in the question.

2. Restate the question

Turn the question into the beginning of a statement. Do not copy the question word for word.

3. Add your answer

Complete the statement with your answer, conclusion, or position.

See an example

Question: Why does the narrator feel embarrassed in Fish Cheeks?

Restated question: In Fish Cheeks, the narrator feels embarrassed because...

Complete claim: In Fish Cheeks, the narrator feels embarrassed because she worries that Robert will judge her family and their traditions.

Choose a beginning only when it fits your question:

Before moving on, check that your claim:
  • uses important words or ideas from the question,
  • turns the question into a statement, and
  • includes your actual answer or conclusion.
